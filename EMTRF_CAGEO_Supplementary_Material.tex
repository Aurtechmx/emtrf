\section*{Supplementary Methods}

\noindent Three drivers are labelled reconstructions because the original scripts were not preserved: the ablation (the P0--P3 rows of main-text Table~3, Fig.~7 and Table~S2), the polyline bootstrap (the intervals of main-text Fig.~5) and the real-contour timing (Methods S4). Each reproduces its reported values (\texttt{docs/RECONSTRUCTED\_DRIVERS.md}).

\noindent Where a section says its rules were fixed or written before a run, they were written down before the driver was first run. The repository commits rules and results together, so this is a statement of procedure, not an externally timestamped registration.


\noindent\textbf{S1. Adversarial source-arc fixtures.} For each $\varepsilon\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.20,0.40\}$, 2{,}000 deterministic 64-vertex polylines were generated from a low-amplitude sinusoidal geometry (10 units long, amplitude 0.01--0.19 units, so $\varepsilon$ is in the same units) with a fixed mulberry32 seed family (seed \texttt{0xC0FFEE}$+$index). Source supports were sampled from $[0.78,0.98)$; one to four non-endpoint vertices were then replaced by weak support in $[0.08,0.50)$. Ordinary DP and EC-DP used the same geometric recursion. For every simplified span the endpoint-only value was the minimum of its retained endpoints (support is attached to vertices here; in the field runs it is attached to edges, and the endpoint record is that of the first and last replaced edges), and the contract value was the minimum across the complete represented source arc; an endpoint-only promotion was counted when the former exceeded the latter, and a geometry mismatch when the retained index sets differed on fully supported fixtures. A separate gap experiment generated 1{,}000 fixtures, marked one interior vertex unsupported, and simplified at $\varepsilon=0.25$; a baseline bridge was a DP segment whose retained endpoints lay on opposite sides of the unsupported vertex, whereas EC-DP split the source at that vertex so any crossing segment would violate the contract. A closed-ring extension used a deterministic 32-vertex ring with one weak interior value: when no semantic boundary supplies a start, the reference rule selects the lexicographically smallest $(x,y)$ vertex, rotates the ring to that anchor, duplicates it to unroll the cycle, applies EC-DP, and re-closes the output, with complete-source evidence including the cyclic interval adjacent to the cut. All 32 cyclic reindexings were required to produce one canonical output signature.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S2. Real-data block-recovery audit.} For each executed crop the study called the frozen production rasterization, confidence-aware DTM, and contour modules. Each emitted marching-squares segment lies within one known $2\times2$ DTM-cell block (ambiguous saddle cells are resolved by the asymptotic decider of Nielson and Hamann, 1991, Proceedings of Visualization '91, 83--91, doi:10.1109/VISUAL.1991.175782); the audit recovered that producing block geometrically and required its recomputed four-corner minimum confidence to match the emitted segment confidence. Source provenance was reconstructed from the four cell states, stored independently of visual grade: $M$ when all four cells were measured-derived, $I$ when all four were interpolated-derived, and $X$ when both occurred. Unsupported corners cannot produce a segment because the contour extractor already refuses such cells. The modules are a frozen study snapshot, not byte-identical to the audited v0.6.5 release: the snapshot leaves unreachable voids unsupported unless a bounded fallback is authorized, whereas the release fills them from the nearest support. This changes the supported domain and the contour population, so main-text Table~2 reproduces from the snapshot only; the export-label defect is independent of it and reproduces against the release.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S3. Standalone study components.} The study is independent of the production application. It contains a JavaScript implementation of the reference support functions, directional classifier, ordinary DP, EC-DP, deterministic fixture generation, unsupported-gap injection, cyclic-ring handling, and timing harness. One independently written Python program checks the typed-evidence algebra, including the applicability, scope and lineage clauses: a channel that is not applicable to a source is ignored, whereas a channel applicable to a required source but carrying no value makes the output channel unavailable rather than computed from the remaining sources (a refusal preserved under composition), output scope is the intersection of the applicable sources' scopes, and lineage is the complete union over the source set. A property test checks composition directly (rules written beforehand: \texttt{docs/\allowbreak RULES\_\allowbreak ADDITIONAL\_\allowbreak TESTS.md}, D11): in 10\,000 random cases (2--9 source records, each channel a value, applicable but missing, or not applicable, with or without a declared scope), meeting consecutive groups and then the group results gave the same record as one meet over all sources in every case (32\,489 groups; 0 mismatches). A deliberately broken composition that drops an inherited unavailable state fails 1\,866 of 2\,000 such cases. Driver: \texttt{oracles/composition\_property.py}. The same test on the JavaScript \texttt{typedMeet} that EC-DP and EC-wrap use (D13, rules written beforehand) gave no mismatch in 10\,000 cases (32\,703 groups; 8\,663 channel results carried a value, the rest were unavailable or absent), and its broken control was caught in 1\,860 of 2\,000 cases. Driver: \texttt{js-study/composition\_property.cjs}. The same six clauses are also asserted against the shipped implementation by the exact conformance checks; a second Python program independently reimplements ordinary DP and EC-DP and is evaluated differentially on the same deterministic fixture coordinates and supports rather than by calling JavaScript output. Raw results, environment metadata, and checksums are frozen with the study artifacts.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S4. Timing protocol.} For the synthetic benchmark, after five warm-ups ordinary DP and EC-DP were timed in 30 paired repetitions with alternating execution order, and retained-geometry checksums were required to match. The four-site real-contour benchmark used a site-normalized tolerance of one source grid cell (1.0 m for White Sands, Estonia Tava, and StREAM; 0.5 m for Marsh Island) over 289 polylines and 5{,}305 source vertices per pass (the corpus of the reconstructed timing driver; the other four-crop analyses use the 237 feature lines, 3{,}452 vertices and 3{,}215 edges of Methods S7); because one pass is sub-millisecond, each timed repetition batched 100 identical passes with five warm-ups and 30 alternating paired repetitions, then divided by 100. This is a post-contour representation-stage benchmark and excludes point loading, DTM construction, contour extraction, browser rendering, and GPU work. Median kernel time was 45.49 ms for DP and 59.03 ms for EC-DP on the 640{,}000 synthetic vertices (ratio 1.30), and the ratio was 1.032 on the four crops; Node.js timings on one machine.

\noindent\emph{Module reachability and test coverage.} The shipped \texttt{simplifyPolyline} path pins vertices below the
solid-confidence floor and vertices adjacent to grade transitions. The separate \texttt{contourAdaptiveGeneralize.ts}
module likewise delegates geometry to per-feature DP, but its own source documentation states it has no production
caller, so it does not affect exported contour evidence. In the audited release,
evidence-registry and evidence-monotonicity tests reject authority upgrades in registered derivation chains and the E4
studies check selected numerical operators, whereas the adaptive-generalization tests exercise tolerance selection and
geometry immutability/retention, not complete-source provenance/support serialization. Test reachability and operator
conformance are therefore separate claims.


\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S6. Cross-implementation validation context (E4).} The audited source defines E4 as agreement with a second implementation of the same computation, separate from E5 field ground truth. Two E4 records bear on the upstream geometry used in the main text: the mean 1~m DTM on the Estonia crop against a SciPy binned point-in-cell mean, and marching-squares isolines against GDAL on an analytic DEM. Both are author-run implementation-agreement cross-checks, not evidence that export provenance is correct, and not field-accuracy validation.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S7. Alternative methods.} All methods receive the same input: the strict feature runs of the four crops (contours at one-cell intervals, joined, split at display-grade changes), with per-edge typed evidence, and the production DP distance rule at a one-cell tolerance. \emph{DP}: ordinary DP; each span carries the evidence of its first and last source edge. \emph{Length-weighted mean}: DP geometry; each span carries, per channel, the length-weighted mean support of the edges it replaces that have the channel, and the provenance union of those edges. \emph{Boundary vertices}: a vertex is forced wherever the provenance class ($M$, $I$, $X$) changes between adjacent edges, and DP is applied between forced vertices with endpoint attribution. \emph{Homogeneous splitting ($b$)}: the line is cut wherever provenance or typed support changes, with support compared after integer division by $b$ points on the 0--100 scale ($b=0$ means exact equality; $b=10,25$), and DP is applied to each run with endpoint attribution. \emph{EC-DP}: DP geometry; each span carries the minimum typed support and provenance union of every edge it replaces. For every method a span is promoted when the support it carries exceeds the minimum over its replaced edges in either channel, and loses provenance when its carried provenance misses a class present in those edges. Length-weighted over- and understatement compare the span's carried support with each replaced edge's own value. Driver: \texttt{ablation/baselines.cjs}.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S8. Alternative support profiles.} Each profile replaces the per-cell support field (0--100) before contour attribution; geometry is unchanged. Parameters were fixed before any run and not tuned. \emph{Exponential}: measured cells $100(1-e^{-n/2})$; interpolated cells $50\,e^{-(d-1)/1.5}$, with $n$ the return count and $d$ the distance to data in cells. \emph{Kriging}: $100(1-\sigma^2/s)$ with sill $s=1$, where $\sigma^2$ is the simple-kriging (Gaussian-process posterior) variance at the cell from its $k=8$ nearest measured cells within 6 cells, with exponential covariance (sill $s=1$, range 3 cells) and observation noise $\tau^2/n_j$, $\tau^2=0.5$; the computation was checked against an independent NumPy solution on six cells. \emph{Standard error} (named SURVEY in the code; no survey checkpoints are used): $100\exp(-\mathrm{se}^2/(2\cdot0.10^2))$, where se is the standard error of the cell elevation in metres: $\sqrt{(s_z^2+0.05^2)/n}$ for measured cells ($s_z$ the sample standard deviation of the cell's returns, set to 0 for a single-return cell, whose $\mathrm{se}$ is then the instrument term alone; 0.05 m instrument precision) and $\sqrt{\mathrm{se}_{\mathrm{med}}^2+(0.05\,d\,L)^2}$ for interpolated cells ($\mathrm{se}_{\mathrm{med}}$ the median measured se, $L$ the cell size). Results per site are in Table~S7. Driver: \texttt{ablation/support\_profiles.cjs}.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S9. Third-party toolchains.} Six chains rebuild the DTM, contours and simplification from the committed returns; no chain runs OLV or PDAL code. GDAL 3.13.3: \texttt{gdal\_rasterize -add} (sum and count), \texttt{gdal raster calc}, \texttt{fill-nodata}, \texttt{proximity}, \texttt{gdal\_contour}, \texttt{gdal vector simplify} (GEOS topology-preserving DP). GRASS 8.5.0: \texttt{r.in.xyz} (mean, n), \texttt{r.fillnulls} (bilinear), \texttt{r.grow.distance}, \texttt{r.contour}, \texttt{v.generalize method=douglas}. SAGA 7.8.2: Shapes to Grid (mean, count), Close Gaps, Proximity Grid, Contour Lines from Grid, Line Simplification. WhiteboxTools 2.4.0: VectorPointsToRaster (sum, number), FillMissingData, EuclideanDistance, ContoursFromRaster (no smoothing or generalization); lines simplified with scikit-image \texttt{approximate\_polygon}, since WhiteboxTools has no line simplifier. R 4.6.1: lidR 4.3.3 \texttt{pixel\_metrics} and \texttt{rasterize\_terrain} (TIN), terra \texttt{distance}, isoband \texttt{isolines}, sf \texttt{st\_simplify}. Python: NumPy binning, SciPy \texttt{griddata} (linear) and \texttt{distance\_transform\_edt}, scikit-image \texttt{find\_contours} and \texttt{approximate\_polygon}. Contour interval and simplification tolerance equal the cell size.

A script then reads each tool's own outputs. Every source edge is assigned the $2\times2$ block of cell centres containing its midpoint, and the script checks that the block brackets the contour level (all 23{,}566 edges did). Provenance is $M$, $I$ or $X$ from the block's return counts; the measured channel is the smallest return count among measured cells and the interpolation channel the largest distance to data among empty cells. Simplified lines are matched to their source lines by level, end vertices and vertex subsequence, with backtracking where a line passes through the same point twice; every simplified line matched. Because only the order of counts and distances is used, the promotion counts hold for any support function strictly increasing in return count and strictly decreasing in distance; a function with plateaus, saturation or rounding can merge values and change them.

The tools bin returns by four rules, recorded per chain in the output: half-open cells counted from the south (NumPy; the production code also keeps returns lying exactly on the outer grid boundary, which differs from NumPy in 4 cells at White Sands and Estonia); a return exactly on a horizontal cell edge assigned to the cell above (GDAL, GRASS, WhiteboxTools); the same after rounding coordinates to the 0.001 LAS scale and keeping returns on the outer grid boundary (lidR); and half-open cells restricted to the extent between outer cell centres (SAGA). Re-binning the returns with each rule reproduces that tool's cell counts in every cell at every site. Three WhiteboxTools 2.4.0 defects were worked around: \texttt{VectorPointsToRaster} fails with \texttt{assign=mean} or \texttt{--nodata}, so the mean is sum divided by number; its ASCII export writes the lower-left corner one cell too far north, so GeoTIFF outputs are read and their georeference checked; and the Python wrapper reports success when a tool fails, so every output file is checked. Per-chain results are in Table~S8. Driver: \texttt{toolchain-audit/reproduce.sh}.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S10. Worked example and attribute query.} Display grade comes from vertex support, a segment taking the weaker of its two vertices, while evidence records take the minimum over each segment's 2$\times$2 block; the two can differ, as in the worked span. The query keeps a span when its exported measured support is at least $t$ and its exported provenance is $\{M\}$, for $t=66$ (solid) and $t=33$ (dashed). The reference answer applies the same test to each source edge. False acceptance is the length of source edges that fail the test inside spans the query accepts; over-rejection is the length of source edges that pass inside spans it rejects. The traced span is selected by a fixed rule: among DP spans whose endpoint-attributed record passes the query at $t=66$ and promotes support, the largest promotion, then the longer span, then site order. Seven spans qualified. The traced span is 4.81 m long. Its weak cell holds 2 returns (site median 12), both near its eastern edge, so its support is $C_M(2;12,3)=0.07$; three of the five source segments, 2.62 m in all, depend on that cell. The map draws the stretch as a gap because display grade comes from a segment's weaker end vertex and every segment here touches the weak cell. The support profile of the span reads 45.5\% of length at or above 66 and 54.5\% below 33. Results per site are in Table~S9: at $t=33$, 4.0--50.8\% of the length DP accepted failed at source level, and EC-DP accepted no failing length. Driver: \texttt{ablation/worked\_example.cjs}.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S11. Directional classifier and closed rings.} Directional support is evaluated on eight rays around a cell. A pattern is one-sided when it has a single occupied direction or when the largest angular gap between adjacent occupied rays exceeds four of eight sectors. Because the rule depends on cyclic gaps rather than compass labels, it is invariant under 45-degree rotation; all 256 patterns are verified exhaustively. In the reference implementation (S1), closed rings are cut at the lexicographically smallest $(x,y)$ vertex; in the field-run drivers a ring starts at the first vertex of the stitcher's output, identically for every method. \emph{Chords and unsupported cells.} EC-DP splits a line at unsupported source segments, so no simplified segment replaces one. This does not by itself prevent a chord from passing over an unsupported cell that its source segments went around, and geometry equivalence with DP holds whether or not it does. The case was counted: each one-cell DP chord was tested by exact segment--cell intersection against every cell with no supported elevation; passing through the interior of such a cell counts, touching only its edge or corner does not. On the two crops that have such cells (White Sands, 32\% of cells; Marsh Island, 49\%), 0 of 268 chords passed through one; Estonia Tava and StREAM have none to cross. This is an observation on these crops at this tolerance, not a property of the method. A pipeline that needs the stronger guarantee must test each chord against the mask and keep the vertices of a chord that fails. Driver: \texttt{ablation/chord\_mask.cjs}.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{S12. EC-wrap.} Given a source line with one evidence record per edge and a simplifier's output whose vertices are a subsequence of the source vertices with both ends kept, EC-wrap recovers the source indices of the output vertices (in order; with backtracking where a line passes through the same point twice; if the retained vertices match the source in more than one way, the line is refused and reported, not guessed, because the replaced edges would then differ between mappings) and assigns each output segment the complete-source minimum per channel, the provenance union and the support profile of the edges between consecutive retained indices. The simplifier is a black box. The matching is the one used for the six toolchains (Methods S9). As a non-Douglas--Peucker demonstration, the strict feature runs of the four crops were simplified by Visvalingam--Whyatt (\texttt{simplification} 2.0.0, Rust implementation) at an effective area of one source cell squared; every output line matched its source. Smoothing is not a subsequence operator, so EC-wrap does not apply to it; the reference smoothing operator gives each new edge the minimum and provenance union of the parent edges it spans (\texttt{smoothEvidence} in \texttt{js-study/reference/typedEvidenceOperators.ts}), and EC-DP then acts on the smoothed line. Simplified vertices are matched to source vertices by coordinates rounded to $10^{-9}$ m. A channel applies to an edge by its provenance (measured to $M$ and $X$, interpolation to $I$ and $X$); if an applicable edge has no value, the output channel is unavailable (rule iv). The command-line tool \texttt{tools/ecwrap\_cli.py} writes these records to GeoJSON at full precision, with a state field per channel (value, unavailable or not applicable), so a query on the file gives the same answer as on the source edges; \texttt{run\_all.sh} checks this on a minimum of 65.96 queried at 66 and on a missing applicable value. Driver: \texttt{ablation/ecwrap.py} with \texttt{ablation/export\_runs.cjs}.

\noindent\textbf{S13. Horizontal position uncertainty.} Rules fixed before the run. Each cell's vertical standard error $\mathrm{se}$ is that of the standard-error profile (Methods S8). For the $2\times2$ block of a contour edge, the horizontal position uncertainty is $u=\max(\mathrm{se})/\max(|\nabla z|,\,0.05)$, with $|\nabla z|$ the magnitude of the block's bilinear gradient at the block centre (corner elevations differenced over the cell size in metres) and 0.05 m/m a floor that keeps flat ground finite. \noindent\textbf{S28. \section*{Supplementary Notes}

\noindent\textbf{N1. Worked substitution of the reference support profile.} The measured and interpolated
reference support functions (main-text Eqs.~5--6) are conformance profiles, not part of the EMTRF contract, and
may be replaced by any monotone support model without affecting provenance, applicability, lineage, or EC-DP
geometry. As a concrete example, suppose an interpolation method supplies a kriging standard error $\sigma_K$ and
defines a new channel $S_K=\sigma_0/(\sigma_0+\sigma_K)$ with $\sigma_0>0$, applicable only to
interpolated-derived cells. For $\sigma_0=0.25$~m and a source arc with $\sigma_K=\{0.10,0.25,0.40\}$~m, the
channel values are approximately $\{0.714,0.500,0.385\}$ and the minimum over the arc is $0.385$. Replacing the
cell-level formula changes only how that channel is built; the provenance union, the rule that no output replaces an unsupported source segment,
applicability, lineage, and EC-DP geometry are unchanged. Conformance to the contract therefore does not depend on the choice of support function. Observed promotion counts are
preserved only by strictly order-preserving transformations of the tested quantity; saturation or rounding can change them.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{N2. Proofs.} \emph{Proposition 1.} For a fixed channel $\mu$ with every applicable source supplying a value, $S_y(\mu)=\min_{x\in D_y^{\mu}}S_x(\mu)$ is bounded above by each $S_x(\mu)$ by definition of the minimum, and commutativity, associativity and idempotence are those of $\min$ on $[0,1]$. Unlike channels are never combined, and a missing applicable value is not skipped: by rule (iv) the channel is unavailable.

\emph{Proposition 2.} EC-DP runs the same recursion and distance test as ordinary DP and attaches evidence only after the retained index set is fixed, so on a fully supported line at fixed tolerance, with no caller-supplied protected indices, the retained sets coincide. The equality is also asserted executably, including across provenance transitions.

\emph{Proposition 3.} For segment $j$ and applicable channel $\mu$ with complete values, EC-DP assigns $\min_{k\in D_j^{\mu}}S_k(\mu)$, which is at most $S_k(\mu)$ for every applicable $k$ on the replaced arc; with an applicable value missing it emits no number.

\emph{Theorem 1.} Consider operators $T_1,\ldots,T_r$, each obeying rules (i)--(vi), and an output $z$ of the chain with ancestor set $D_z^{*}$ (sources of sources, by composition of the per-operator source maps). (i) Union is associative, so the provenance of $z$ is the exact union over $D_z^{*}$; nothing is removed or invented. (ii) If any required ancestor is unsupported, the operator receiving it emits no supported element across it, so no later operator receives one. This step assumes that each source map records the unsupported interval, so that a later operator joining the elements on either side counts that interval among its required ancestors; an operator that drops it without a trace breaks the chain and does not conform. (iii) For channel $\mu$, each operator emits either no value or a value bounded by every applicable input value; by transitivity the final value is bounded by every applicable ancestor value. Sources for which $\mu$ is not applicable are excluded at the step where they enter and so never bound or contribute. A channel applies to an output exactly when it applies to at least one of its required sources; if it applies to none, the output carries no value and no applicability for $\mu$, so at later steps it neither contributes a value nor blocks one, and any later value is bounded by the applicable ancestors that do carry $\mu$. (iv) An unavailable channel at step $s$ is, for step $s+1$, an applicable source with a missing value, so rule (iv) at step $s+1$ keeps it unavailable; by induction it stays unavailable to the end of the chain and is never replaced by a value computed from other sources. (v) Scope is an intersection at each step, and repeated intersection cannot broaden it. (vi) Lineage at each step identifies the complete source set, so composing the maps identifies $D_z^{*}$.

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{N3. Synthetic-surface GDAL audit.} Contours from GDAL \texttt{gdal\_contour} 3.13.1 were extracted at 0.5 m from a deterministic analytic DEM and scored against a reference support surface that decays with distance from two measurement passes. The output schema carried elevation and identifier fields but no provenance, support, applicability or lineage field. Endpoint-only inheritance promoted 18 of 91 spans (19.8\%); complete-source inheritance promoted none. The audit runs in the reproduction gate (\texttt{gdal-audit/}); the main text reports the six-toolchain audit on the four field crops instead (Methods S9).

\vspace{4mm}
\noindent\textbf{N4. Support-profile record.} For a span representing source edges with lengths $l_k$ and channel values $v_k$, the profile stores $f_t=\sum_{k:v_k\ge t} l_k/L$, with $L=\sum_k l_k$ taken over the edges to which the channel applies, for each display threshold $t\in\{66,33\}$, beside $\min_k v_k$. Here $v_k$ is on the experimental 0--100 scale, so thresholds 66 and 33 correspond to 0.66 and 0.33 in the normalized contract. Edges to which the channel does not apply are left out of both sums, so $L$ is channel-specific; write $L_{j,\mu}$ for it on span $j$ and channel $\mu$, recoverable from the lineage interval and the applicability of the source edges, or stored as a fourth field. The fractions are exact only when no applicable edge has a missing value: such an edge would make the qualifying length undetermined, and the profile should then be marked unavailable or report known and unknown length separately. The tested implementation is restricted to an input contract in which a null channel value means the channel does not apply, never applicable but unavailable; the driver counts applicable edges with a null value and the gate requires zero (0 of 3,215 edges at the four sites). The applicable-but-unavailable case is therefore specified but not exercised. Spans with no applicable source length contribute nothing to a merge; if the combined applicable length is zero, the profile is marked inapplicable and no numeric fraction is emitted. Merging spans $j$ gives $f_{t,\mu}=\sum_j L_{j,\mu}f_{t,j,\mu}/\sum_j L_{j,\mu}$, weighted by channel-applicable length, exactly when the merged spans partition the represented source length, as consecutive EC-DP spans do; spans that share source edges need those edges counted once. Weighting by total represented length does not generally preserve exactness when the proportion of applicable source length differs between spans: in the composition check, 341 of 708 merges include edges to which the channel does not apply, and total-length weights give a wrong value in 33 of 1,416 span--channel--threshold checks (and a value in 412 where the channel applies to no edge), while applicable-length weights give none. Under the stated condition the record is exact under composition and cannot overstate at any step. The profile gives the total source length at or above a threshold in one channel. It does not say where along the chord that length lies, and it does not answer a query that also tests provenance: at White Sands and $t=33$ the measured channel has 241.52 m at or above the threshold, of which 237.46 m is on measured-only edges (Tables S9, S11). Scalar bound: a single record $r$ safe for every threshold query must satisfy $r\le\min_k v_k$, otherwise the query at any $t\in(\min_k v_k,\,r]$ accepts the span while the minimum edge fails it. The driver \texttt{ablation/evidence\_profile.cjs} checks all three statements on the four crops: profile length accounting exact in every site and channel (Table~S11); two-cell spans equal to the applicable-length-weighted merge of their one-cell spans in 708 of 708 span--channel checks; and an unsafe threshold found for all 53 DP spans whose endpoint record exceeds the arc minimum. An endpoint record whose two end edges do not carry a channel states nothing for that channel. Table~S11 gives net lengths. Summed span by span, endpoint records understated the measured channel by up to 38.40 m and overstated it by 12.76 m at Estonia Tava ($t=33$; net 25.64 m); the largest overstatement was 16.28 m, at StREAM ($t=33$). The record needs three numeric attribute fields per channel, plus the applicable length unless it is recovered from lineage.
